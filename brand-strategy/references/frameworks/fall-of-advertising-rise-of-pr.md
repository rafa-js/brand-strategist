# The Fall of Advertising & the Rise of PR
### Al Ries & Laura Ries (2002)

> *"Advertising cannot start a fire. It can only fan a fire after it has been started by public relations."*

---

## Core Premise

For most of the 20th century, advertising was considered the engine of brand building. Ries & Ries argue this belief is now fundamentally wrong. Advertising has become too expensive, too cluttered, and too incredible to build a new brand. **PR — earned media, third-party credibility, editorial coverage — is the only force that can launch a brand from zero.** Advertising then serves a maintenance role, not a launch role.

---

## Key Highlights

### 1. The Credibility Gap
- Advertising is paid speech. Consumers know it and discount it automatically.
- PR is third-party endorsement — editorial, press, word-of-mouth, influencer. It arrives with **borrowed credibility**.
- A review in a trusted publication or a recommendation from a respected voice does what a full-page ad cannot: it makes the claim believable.

### 2. Advertising Cannot Build a Brand From Zero
- To build a brand, you must first establish it in the prospect's mind as something real, new, and credible.
- Advertising amplifies awareness of an already-established brand — it cannot create the initial belief.
- New brands launched on advertising alone (without PR foundations) almost always fail or require massive ongoing spend to sustain awareness artificially.

### 3. The PR-First Strategy
- Successful modern brands almost always begin with a PR moment: a news story, a product review, a celebrity mention, a viral event.
- **Examples of PR-built brands**: Starbucks, Amazon, Google, Red Bull, The Body Shop, Walmart — none were primarily advertising-built in their formative years.
- Advertising followed *after* the brand was established in the public consciousness.

### 4. The Role Reversal
| Function | Old Model | New Model (Ries) |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Launch a brand | Advertising | PR |
| Build credibility | Advertising claims | Editorial coverage |
| Maintain awareness | PR stories | Advertising repetition |
| Measure success | Reach & frequency | Share of voice in earned media |

### 5. The "Word" Still Needs PR to Stick
- Even if a brand has a perfectly focused position (see *Positioning*), that word cannot take hold in the mind unless it is validated by independent third parties.
- Advertising says "We are the best." PR says "Others say they are the best." Minds believe the latter.

### 6. The Decline of Advertising Effectiveness
- Ad clutter has exploded; attention spans have collapsed.
- DVRs, ad-blockers, streaming subscriptions, banner blindness — consumers have built structural defenses against advertising.
- The cost-per-impression in advertising rises while the credibility-per-impression falls.

### 7. PR Must Be "Newsworthy," Not Just Positive
- The biggest mistake in PR is trying to get coverage for non-news.
- PR is earned by being genuinely **first, different, controversial, human, or remarkable**.
- "Better" is not news. "First ever," "category-defining," "surprising reversal," or "underdog story" is news.

### 8. The Publicity Ladder
- Top-tier: national broadcast, major newspaper front sections, viral social media moments.
- Mid-tier: trade publications, industry blogs, podcast interviews.
- Bottom-tier: local press, newsletters, branded content masquerading as editorial.
- A PR strategy works upward — start where your brand has the strongest newsworthy angle and build up.

---

## Practical Application Framework

### For a New Brand Launch
1. Identify the one newsworthy angle your brand legitimately owns (first, most extreme, most counterintuitive).
2. Target Tier 1 media that reach your exact prospect.
3. Build the PR story *before* the product launches — create anticipation.
4. Let media coverage generate search, word-of-mouth, and early adopters.
5. Only then layer on advertising to *remind* and *reinforce* — never to *introduce*.

### For an Established Brand
1. Diagnose whether declining sales are a positioning problem (PR must re-establish) or an awareness problem (advertising can help).
2. Refresh PR with a genuinely new angle — not a repackaging of old claims.
3. Ensure advertising is executing the same singular idea PR established. Never let advertising contradict the PR-built brand story.

---

## The Internet Amplifier
- The authors' 2002 arguments were validated and supercharged by social media, SEO, and influencer culture.
- Today, earned media includes: reviews (Amazon, Yelp, App Store), organic social sharing, influencer mentions, podcast citations, and search engine authority.
- The PR-first principle now applies in digital channels: you need others talking about you before you pay to spread the message yourself.

---

## One-Line Summary
> PR builds brands; advertising maintains them. Reverse the order at your peril.
