# Contagious: Why Things Catch On
### Jonah Berger (2013)

> *"Virality isn't born, it's made."*

---

## Core Premise

Word of mouth is the primary driver of 20 to 50 percent of all purchasing decisions, and it beats advertising on both credibility (people trust friends, not ads) and targeting (people share with exactly the friends who care). Contagious content is not a matter of luck, creativity, or budget: it is engineered. Berger's research identifies six principles (STEPPS) that make ideas, products, and stories more likely to be talked about and shared. And because only about 7 percent of word of mouth happens online, the test is not "will people click this" but "will people retell this face to face."

---

## The Six STEPPS

### 1. Social Currency
- People share things that make them look good: smart, in the know, insiders.
- Three levers: **inner remarkability** (find what is surprising, novel, or extreme in the product), **game mechanics** (visible status people earn and want to show off), and **making people feel like insiders** (scarcity and exclusivity).
- Example: Please Don't Tell, the New York bar hidden behind a phone booth inside a hot dog shop. The secret is the marketing; people share secrets precisely because secrets are social currency.

### 2. Triggers
- Top of mind means tip of tongue. People talk about whatever the environment reminds them of, and immediate word of mouth needs a frequent cue.
- Link the brand to a cue people encounter often in the moments that matter. The cue does the advertising long after the message is gone.
- Examples: Kit Kat tied itself to coffee breaks ("a break's best friend") and revived the brand; Rebecca Black's "Friday" spiked in searches every Friday.
- An interesting message without a trigger gets shared once. An ordinary message with a strong trigger gets shared every time the cue appears.

### 3. Emotion
- When we care, we share. But the driver is arousal, not positivity: **high-arousal emotions (awe, excitement, amusement, anger, anxiety) increase sharing; low-arousal emotions (sadness, contentment) suppress it.**
- Don't list features; find the feeling. Ask "why does this matter" three times until you hit something people care about.
- Example: Google's "Parisian Love" ad turned search queries into a love story and was shared widely; a feature list never would have been.

### 4. Public
- Built to show, built to grow. Behavior that is observable gets imitated; private behavior cannot spread.
- Design for visibility: make the product advertise itself, and create **behavioral residue**, physical evidence that persists after the action.
- Examples: Apple flipped the laptop logo so it reads correctly to onlookers; Movember turned a private donation into a public mustache; Livestrong's wristband outlived the campaign.

### 5. Practical Value
- News you can use. People share useful things to help others; usefulness is altruistic social currency.
- Highlight incredible value (frame discounts against the right reference point; the Rule of 100: under 100 dollars, state percentage off; over, state dollars off) and package knowledge so it is easy to pass along.
- Example: Ken Craig's clean-the-corn video, an 86-year-old's kitchen tip, reached millions because it saves people a real annoyance.

### 6. Stories
- Information travels under the cover of idle chatter. People do not share facts; they tell stories, and the lesson rides inside.
- Build a **Trojan horse**: a narrative people want to retell in which the brand's idea is structurally integral, not incidental.
- **The Trojan horse test: if the story can be retold without the brand or its core claim, the virality is wasted.** Blendtec's "Will It Blend" passes (you cannot tell the story without the blender's power); Evian's roller-skating babies failed (50 million views, sales down, because the babies carry the story and the water does not).

---

## Application Framework: Engineering a Contagious PR Narrative

1. **Score the core story and the lead angle against all six STEPPS.** Strong on two or three is normal; weak on Emotion or Stories means rewrite, not proceed.
2. **Find the inner remarkability.** What about the brand's claim is genuinely surprising? That is the headline energy and the social currency.
3. **Attach a trigger.** Tie the message to a frequent cue in the prospect's week (a moment, a ritual, a recurring frustration) so the conversation restarts without media spend.
4. **Raise the arousal.** Reframe the story until it produces awe, righteous anger, anxiety, or amusement. Evidence supplies credibility; emotion supplies motion.
5. **Make participation visible.** Give early users and influencers something observable: an artifact, a badge, a public behavior that leaves residue.
6. **Package practical value.** Ship a genuinely useful one-pager, tool, or rule of thumb that travels on its own and carries the brand with it.
7. **Run the Trojan horse test before launch.** Retell the story without naming the brand. If the core claim survives only when the brand is present, the story is doing its job.

---

## Where This Sits in the Methodology

STEPPS is subordinate to the position. It makes the locked story travel; it never picks the story. The verbal nail and the PR-first sequence come first (Positioning, Fall of Advertising and Rise of PR), and shareability is then engineered into the narrative, the angles, and the influencer activation. If a highly shareable idea conflicts with the word the brand owns, drops credibility below the evidence, or blurs the category, the nail wins and the idea is cut. Virality that does not encode the position is Evian's babies: applause without sales.

---

## One-Line Summary

> People don't share ads. They share remarkable, emotional, useful stories that make them look good, and the brand must be the part they cannot leave out.
