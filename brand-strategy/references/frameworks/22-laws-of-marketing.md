# The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing
### Al Ries & Jack Trout (1993)

> *"Violating the laws of marketing will harm your brand. Violating them with a big budget will destroy it faster."*

---

## Core Premise

Marketing does not operate by intuition or trend. It operates by **laws** — consistent, repeatable principles grounded in how human minds categorize information. Knowing the laws won't guarantee success, but **breaking them virtually guarantees failure**. This book is a diagnostic manual as much as a strategy guide.

---

## The 22 Laws — Summary & Insight

### Laws of Leadership & Pioneering
1. **Law of Leadership** — It's better to be first than to be better. The goal is to be first *in the mind*, not the market.
2. **Law of the Category** — If you can't be first in a category, create a new category where you can be first.
3. **Law of the Mind** — Being first in the mind beats being first to market. Perception is the only reality that matters.

### Laws of Perception & Reality
4. **Law of Perception** — Marketing is not a battle of products; it's a battle of perceptions.
5. **Law of Focus** — The most powerful concept in marketing is owning a single word in the prospect's mind.
6. **Law of Exclusivity** — Two companies cannot own the same word in the prospect's mind. Don't try to take someone else's word.

### Laws of the Ladder
7. **Law of the Ladder** — The strategy you use depends on which rung you occupy on the ladder. Rules differ for #1, #2, and #3 brands.
8. **Law of Duality** — In the long run, every market becomes a two-horse race. Plan for it.
9. **Law of the Opposite** — If you're the #2 brand, your strategy is dictated by the leader. Be the anti-leader, not a clone.
10. **Law of Division** — Over time, a category will divide into two or more categories. Ride the new category early.

### Laws of Perspective & Time
11. **Law of Perspective** — Marketing effects take place over an extended period of time. Short-term sales gains often destroy long-term positioning.
12. **Law of Line Extension** — The most violated law. Extending a brand name into adjacent categories destroys its original position. **This is where most large companies fail.**
13. **Law of Sacrifice** — You must give something up in order to get something. Narrow your product line, target, and message — don't try to be everything.

### Laws of Attributes & Candor
14. **Law of Attributes** — For every attribute, there is an opposite, effective attribute. Don't imitate the leader — find a contrasting attribute to own.
15. **Law of Candor** — When you admit a negative, the prospect will give you a positive. Honesty disarms skepticism and builds trust instantly.
16. **Law of Singularity** — In each situation, only one bold stroke will produce substantial results. Marketing is not a battle of efforts — find the single point of leverage.

### Laws of Success, Failure & Hype
17. **Law of Unpredictability** — Unless you write your competitor's plans, you can't predict the future. Build flexibility, not rigid forecasts.
18. **Law of Success** — Success often leads to arrogance, and arrogance to failure. Winners start believing their own press.
19. **Law of Failure** — Failure is to be expected and accepted. Cut losses early — don't rescue failed ideas with more resources.
20. **Law of Hype** — The situation is often the opposite of the way it appears in the press. Real revolutions arrive quietly.

### Laws of Acceleration & Resources
21. **Law of Acceleration** — Successful programs are not built on fads, they're built on trends. Ride trends, not fads.
22. **Law of Resources** — Without adequate funding, an idea won't get off the ground. A great idea needs money to work — positioning lives in minds, and reaching minds costs.

## Quick Diagnostic: Is Your Strategy Breaking a Law?

| Symptom | Likely Violated Law |
|---------|-------------------|
| Brand stretched across many products | Law of Line Extension (#12) |
| Trying to compete with Coke on cola | Law of Exclusivity (#6) |
| Copying the market leader | Law of the Opposite (#9) |
| Chasing a fad | Law of Acceleration (#21) |
| Over-relying on press coverage | Law of Hype (#20) |
| Winning too fast, losing focus | Law of Success (#18) |

---

## The Most Critical Law: Line Extension (#12)

Ries & Trout devote special emphasis here because it is the most common, most costly, and most preventable mistake in marketing:
- Putting an established brand name on a new product *borrows* equity but *destroys* the original clarity.
- Examples: Crest toothbrushes, Heinz ketchup chips, Levi's suits — all weakened the mother brand.
- The fix: **create a new brand** for a new category, even if it costs more upfront.

## One-Line Summary
> Marketing is governed by unchanging laws. Know them, follow them, or pay for breaking them.
