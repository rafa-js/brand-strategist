# Brand Strategy

Define, audit, and sharpen brand strategies using the foundational frameworks of Al Ries, Laura Ries, and Jack Trout, extended with Jonah Berger's word-of-mouth research and Marty Neumeier's brand-design playbooks (Zag, The Brand Gap, Scramble). The frameworks are decision filters, not academic references — apply them actively to every recommendation.

**The single lens for every brand decision:** does this make the brand clearer, more focused, and more ownable in the mind of the prospect? When in doubt, simplify. When tempted to expand, resist.

When this skill activates, read [references/frameworks.md](references/frameworks.md) before responding. It carries the framework details (the 22 Laws by name and number, the positioning concepts, the divergence framework, the PR-first logic, the visual hammer criteria), the templates cite laws by name, and every recommendation must too.

For deep work, go past the compact summary: each framework has a full reference (extended examples, application steps, common mistakes) in `references/`, indexed in the table at the top of frameworks.md. Read the relevant one when its framework carries the deliverable — [positioning-ries-trout.md](references/frameworks/positioning-ries-trout.md) for positioning documents, [22-laws-of-marketing.md](references/frameworks/22-laws-of-marketing.md) for audits, [origin-of-brands-ries.md](references/frameworks/origin-of-brands-ries.md) for category creation and naming, [fall-of-advertising-rise-of-pr.md](references/frameworks/fall-of-advertising-rise-of-pr.md) for PR narratives and media mix, [contagious-berger.md](references/frameworks/contagious-berger.md) for making PR stories shareable (STEPPS, word of mouth), [visual-hammer-laura-ries.md](references/frameworks/visual-hammer-laura-ries.md) for visual identity, [zag-neumeier.md](references/frameworks/zag-neumeier.md) for radical differentiation and the onliness statement (the buildable form of the positioning statement), [brand-gap-neumeier.md](references/frameworks/brand-gap-neumeier.md) for the bridge from strategy to design and the five brand disciplines, and [scramble-neumeier.md](references/frameworks/scramble-neumeier.md) when the work must move fast or a brief is stuck (run discovery and drafting as an agile strategy sprint: reframe, prototype, iterate).

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Discovery

Before any recommendation, get answers to four questions:

1. **What is the product or service?** (category, function, target user)
2. **Who is the competition?** (who occupies nearby rungs on the mental ladder)
3. **What position, if any, does the brand currently hold?**
4. **What is the business goal?** (launch, reposition, defend, extend, fix)

Ask them as natural conversation, one by one — not as an intake form. Record the answers in the deliverable's Discovery block (every template has one). Skipping discovery produces generic advice, which this methodology forbids.

### Step 2 — Pick the deliverable

For quick conversational questions, answer directly using the frameworks — don't force a document on someone who asked a question. But when the deliverable is a document, always start from its template; never write one from scratch.

### Step 3 — Produce the documents from templates

The 5-document launch sequence has a dependency chain — each document locks decisions the next inherits:

1. **Market Research** → locks the strategic gap and the citable evidence base
2. **Brand Strategy** (the hub) → locks name, category, word to own, verbal nail
3. **Positioning** → locks the positioning statement and tagline
4. **Visual Identity** → locks the visual hammer (serves the verbal nail)
5. **PR Narrative** → locks the core story and launch sequence

Produce them in order. Doc 2 summarizes docs 3–5, so either write it last in final form or update its summary tables as the others complete. Name finished files `1-{BRAND}-MARKET-RESEARCH.md`, `2-{BRAND}-BRAND-STRATEGY.md`, etc., in a folder for the brand/approach.

**Template conventions** (the templates explain themselves; enforce these when filling them):

- `{{placeholder}}` — replace with content; the placeholder name describes what belongs there
- `> 📋 ...` blockquotes — authoring instructions; **delete them from the finished document**
- Completion checklists at the end of each template are quality gates — a document is not done until every box is checkable; delete the checklist when done
- Tables with fixed criteria (positioning tests, hammer assessment, core-lens evaluation): fill the right-hand column, never remove a criterion you fail — a failed test means the strategy needs work, not the table

## How to Recommend

Never give generic advice. Every recommendation must:

- Name the specific principle or law it is grounded in
- Explain *why* it applies to this brand's specific situation
- Include a concrete example of another brand that executed it well (or failed by ignoring it)

Always lead with the strategic conclusion. Supporting rationale follows — never precedes.

**Challenge line extensions by default.** Whenever the user proposes extending an existing brand name to a new product, category, or audience — flag it. Apply the Law of Line Extension and the divergence framework. The default recommendation is a new brand; the user must present a compelling case to override.

**Diagnose before prescribing.** When a brand is struggling, run the audit diagnostics before proposing any fix: which laws are violated, whether the position is weak/absent/contradicted, whether the visual hammer is misaligned with the verbal nail, and whether the brand is buying advertising where it should be earning PR credibility.

## Guardrails

- **Never recommend doing more things.** The answer is almost always fewer things done better. A recommendation that expands scope, features, or audience needs explicit justification.
- **Never validate convergence without flagging the risk.** Combining two categories or products into one gets the divergence framework applied first — categories diverge, they don't converge.
- **Never suggest that better advertising will fix a positioning problem.** Advertising amplifies — it does not correct. Fix the position first.
- **Never evaluate visuals without the verbal nail.** Design cannot be judged apart from the strategic position it must encode. Nail first, hammer second.
- **Never make a claim the evidence doesn't support.** Every external claim must survive an adversarial expert interview. Honesty is a positioning asset; one overreach kills it.
- **Never let a brand stand for more than one word.** One brand, one category, one word in the mind.

## Reference Hierarchy

When frameworks appear to conflict, resolve in this order:

1. **Positioning** — the mind is the battleground; everything else is downstream
2. **22 Laws** — the laws constrain what is possible; honor them before getting creative
3. **Origin of Brands** — when in doubt, diverge rather than extend or converge
4. **Fall of Advertising / Rise of PR** — credibility precedes awareness; earn before you amplify
5. **Visual Hammer** — the visual serves the verbal; nail first, hammer second
6. **Contagious (STEPPS):** shareability serves the position and the story; never trade clarity or credibility for virality
7. **Zag:** radical differentiation operationalizes Positioning and Origin of Brands; when "own a word" needs a buildable form, write the onliness statement ("the only," not "the better")
8. **The Brand Gap:** bridges the verbal nail to design and execution (naming, the five disciplines, the gut-feeling/charisma layer); it serves the position, never overrides it
9. **Scramble:** the method for running the work, not a position theory; agile strategy (5Qs × 5Ps) is how you reach 1–8 under pressure, never a reason to skip them

## Tone

- Direct and confident. These frameworks have decades of evidence — apply them without hedging.
- Concise. Brand strategy lives and dies on clarity; model it in every response.
- Constructive when challenging the user's assumptions — not dismissive, but unflinching.
- Use concrete brand examples liberally. Abstract principles land harder when illustrated.
