# Stoke: PR Launch Narrative

## Core Story

> The most popular food apps ask one question: how many calories? It is the right question for the job they were built for, weight loss, and the category leader, MyFitnessPal, now tracks GLP-1 doses too. But a different crowd has filled the start pens. HYROX reports more than 1.5 million participants last season, and 1.34 million people applied to run the 2027 London Marathon. For them the science asks something else: the sports-nutrition position stand says athletes should eat "in relation to sport rather than general daily targets," and the makers of Nutri-Score say their grade "cannot be used" for sport nutrition. The gap shows up on race day. In one 2025 study only 5.3% of marathoners hit their carbohydrate targets; in another, amateurs given a fueling plan ran 4.7% faster. Stoke is a food tracker that drops the calorie budget, scores what you eat against the session you have next, and hands you one swap. It's not a diet. It's fuel.

### Why This Story Is Newsworthy

| Criteria | How Stoke Delivers |
|----------|--------------------|
| **The hook** | "Your food app was built for dieters." It challenges the biggest app in the category, MyFitnessPal, just as it sponsors HYROX, and it liberates millions of amateurs who have been graded on the wrong test. |
| **Candor** | Stoke concedes that calorie counting works for weight loss and that photo logging is imperfect, its own included. It publishes its method and the limits of the evidence, so journalists can quote a startup that says what it cannot prove. |
| **Evidence-backed** | The ACSM/AND/DC joint position (2016), the ISSN protein stand (2017), the IOC consensus on REDs (2023), the NIH photo-logging test (2026, preliminary), Nutri-Score's sport-nutrition exclusion (2025) and amateur fueling studies (Hansen et al., 2014; Smyth, 2021; *Eur J Sport Sci*, 2025). Every claim stays inside them: Stoke says what it is built on, never that it makes anyone faster. |
| **Timely** | HYROX's growth and Infront's September 2026 sale of its majority stake to an L Catterton-led consortium, London's record ballot (May 2026), MyFitnessPal's purchase of Cal AI and its HYROX Tampa sponsorship (2026), GLP-1 drugs reshaping the diet industry, and Apple's iOS 27 food ratings (September 2026). |
| **Universal** | Every amateur has been told a bagel is "bad," stared at a calorie number before a long run, or hit the wall and wondered what went wrong. |

## The Narrative Arc (Three Acts)

**Act 1: The calorie app did its job (concede the status quo)**
"Calorie counting works for what it was built for. If you're trying to lose weight, logging what you eat helps, the trials say so, and the biggest food-tracking app in the world has 280 million members to show for it. Nobody needs to apologize for the calorie."

**Act 2: Then the amateurs showed up (reveal the gap with evidence)**
"But look who's in the start pen now: a million and a half HYROX racers, 1.3 million people trying to get into London, a generation lifting weights. They aren't trying to eat less. They're trying to finish, to lift more, to not hit the wall at mile 20. And the science says their food should change with the session; the official guidelines say 'rather than general daily targets.' A calorie budget can't see tomorrow's long run. The health scores can't either: Nutri-Score's own makers say it was never built for sport. So amateurs get graded on the wrong test, and it shows. In one study, only about one marathoner in twenty hit their race fuel target."

**Act 3: Fuel, not calories (deliver Stoke)**
"Stoke is a food tracker built on the athlete's question. Snap your plate and it scores it for the session you've got next: protein for the lifter, carbohydrate for the long run, both for the hybrid racer. If it falls short, you get one swap, not a meal plan. And it will never tell you to eat less before the work that needs fuel. Fuel, not calories."

Same story at every scale: press pitch, podcast interview, 60-second social video.

## Newsworthy Angles (Ranked)

| Rank | Angle | Headline Energy | Best For |
|------|-------|----------------|----------|
| **1** | "Your food app was built for dieters. You're training for a marathon." | The category mismatch in one line; it challenges the market leader and every amateur recognizes it | National health and science desks, running and fitness media, podcasts |
| **2** | "The bagel paradox: why the 'average' food is exactly right before a long run" | Surprise plus practical value: Nutri-Score grades plain bagels C and honey E, the Australian Institute of Sport lists white bread, jam and honey among food-first race fuels, and the scheme's own owner says it isn't built for sport | Food and health features, social video, newsletters |
| **3** | "1.5 million people race HYROX. No published study has tested how they should fuel." | News of an evidence gap in a race that more than doubled its field in one season, paired with Stoke funding real data | HYROX and functional-fitness media, sports-science outlets, business press covering HYROX's sale |
| **4** | "Amateurs are running on empty" | Data: 5.3% of marathoners hit race carbohydrate targets in one study; 43-47% of recreational endurance samples screen at risk of low energy availability; a fueling plan made amateur marathoners 4.7% faster | Running media, women's health and sports-medicine outlets |
| **5** | "Pros have nutritionists. Amateurs have calorie counters." | A democratization story with the founder at the center | Tech and startup press, founder podcasts |
| **6** | "The food app that tells you to eat more" | Counterintuitive, and it presents the Fuel Floor as an ethic in a category accused of feeding disordered eating | Health and wellbeing desks, sports dietitians' channels |

Lead with #1 and #2: #1 sets the category frame and #2 makes it concrete enough to share. Angle #3 is the one competitors are least placed to run: MyFitnessPal sells HYROX fueling content as a sponsor, and Hexis's elite proof sits in cycling and football. Exploit it by announcing a research partnership on HYROX fueling with a university sports-science lab and publishing open, aggregated data. Angle #6 runs only after the Fuel Floor safety review is complete and only with an advisory-board dietitian as the spokesperson.

## Shareability Check (STEPPS)

| STEPPS Lever | How Stoke's Story Delivers |
|--------------|----------------------------|
| **Social Currency** | Knowing the bagel paradox makes you the person in the run club who understands fueling. The race-week card, with the ember and a Fuel Score, is a badge that says "I prepare properly," and beta access through a coach or club is an insider's ticket. |
| **Triggers** | The night before a long run or heavy session, when the One Swap nudge arrives; race week; Sunday meal prep; the gel in the pocket at km 25; and every time a health app grades a bagel "average." |
| **Emotion** | High-arousal indignation at being graded on a dieter's test ("my app told me off for the breakfast that got me through 30 km"), plus the surprise of the bagel paradox. Never guilt, which is low-arousal and off-brand. |
| **Public** | The ember on shared race-week cards and in Strava activity descriptions; the One Swap posted before race weekends; the "Stoked." card after a personal best. These are visible, persistent residue after the launch. |
| **Practical Value** | One-pagers that travel without the app: "The Bagel Test" (one food, three contexts); "Race fuel per hour" (30-60 g/h for 1-2.5 hours, up to 90 g/h beyond 2.5-3 hours); "Protein per meal" (about 0.25-0.4 g/kg). |
| **Stories** | The archetype: the amateur who "ate clean," bonked at mile 20, then swapped the night-before salad for a rice bowl and finished strong. Food judged by the session ahead is the plot; remove it and the story has no ending. |

**Trojan horse test:** retold without naming Stoke, the story becomes "food apps grade food for dieters; athletes need their food judged by the session ahead." The claim survives, but its ending does not: the listener's next question is "so what judges my food by my session?", and the answer that judges the food itself is Stoke's idea: a score that changes with the session, plus one swap. The bagel paradox in particular cannot be told without that logic: "the same bagel is average on Tuesday and ideal on Sunday" is the Fuel Score in a sentence. Like Blendtec's "Will It Blend?", where the story cannot be told without the blender's power, the product's idea is the plot. **Pass.**

## Influencer Strategy

### Who to Target

| Target | Why | How |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **Sports dietitians (RD, CSSD) active on social** | Their audiences already accept "fuel for the work," and their endorsement transfers clinical authority. Their review also protects the Fuel Floor. | Advisory roles, methodology review, co-authoring the evidence brief; paid for advisory work, never for posts. Their names on the method are the insider credential. |
| **HYROX affiliate-gym coaches and age-group racers** | They live the hybrid fueling problem every week and talk to their members daily; HYROX counts about 15,000 affiliate gyms. | Early access for their members, a race-week fueling clinic kit, first look at the HYROX fueling data. |
| **Run-club leaders** | Clubs are where amateur marathoners learn; Strava counted 1 million clubs in 2025. | Club access codes, long-run fueling nights, the Bagel Test one-pager. |
| **Evidence-minded amateur creators** (marathon, hybrid, lifting) | Credible because they are amateurs with day jobs, and their followers are the tribe. | Access plus the evidence one-pager, and the single request below; their night-before swaps become public artifacts. |

### Who to Avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| **Weight-loss and "what I eat in a day to get lean" creators** | They tie Stoke to the diet paradigm it exists to oppose, and their audiences track for the weight motive most linked to harm. |
| **Clean-eating and anti-ultra-processed purists** | They will attack context-scored gels and bagels; pairing with them invites a "Stoke endorses junk food" story. |
| **Low-carb, keto and carnivore advocates** | The adjacent-posture purists: they reject carbohydrate fueling outright, and the ISSN found ketogenic diets largely neutral or detrimental for performance (Leaf et al., 2024). |
| **Supplement hype accounts** | They undermine credibility, and Stoke sells no supplements; it must not look like a funnel for them. |
| **Paid ambassadors of MyFitnessPal's or Myprotein's HYROX programs** | Conflicted promoters; their posts would read as paid switching. |
| **Elite pros as the face** | It puts Stoke on Hexis's "used by the pros" ladder and away from the amateur tribe. |

### The Influencer Brief

- They receive three months of access, the evidence one-pager ("The Amateur Fuel Gap") and the Fuel Score method.
- The narrative comes as context, not script: why Stoke exists (food judged by the session ahead) and what it will not claim.
- One request: "Before your next long run or heavy session, snap what you actually eat, and if Stoke offers a swap you'd take, tell people whether you took it and how the session went."
- No talking points. No hashtag requirements. The authentic content moment is the night before a hard session and the morning after it.

## What NOT to Do

| Mistake | Why It Fails |
|---------|-------------|
| **Drifting into dieting (the single most dangerous)** | Adding weight goals, a cut mode or "lean" language turns Stoke into a smaller MyFitnessPal. It forfeits "fuel," alienates the people who train for results, and adopts the weight motive most associated with harm among app users. |
| **Leading with AI photo logging** | It puts Stoke on Cal AI's ladder, which MyFitnessPal owns; photo logging is table stakes and free in iOS 27. |
| **Advertising before the claim is established** | "Fuel, not calories" is a contrarian claim that needs third parties (dietitians, journalists, coaches) to make it credible, and MyFitnessPal can outspend any startup. Ads without earned credibility read as a startup shouting. Red Bull and Starbucks were built by publicity first and advertising later. |
| **"Eat like a pro" or "clean fuel" framing** | "Pro" puts Stoke on Hexis's ladder and contradicts the amateur tribe; "clean" puts it on the health-score ladder and moralizes food. |
| **Praising low intake or gamifying restriction** | Under-fueling risk is common in recreational athletes, and athletes carry elevated eating-disorder rates. The Fuel Floor safeguards are launch requirements, not patches. |
| **The overclaim** | "Proven to make you faster," "the most accurate," "the first" or invented HYROX science: one dietitian's quote ends the credibility position, as Cal AI's slide from "90% accurate" to "about 80%" shows. |
| **Ambushing HYROX or its sponsors** | Unofficial event marketing using HYROX marks invites legal and community backlash. Go through coaches, gyms and research instead. |
