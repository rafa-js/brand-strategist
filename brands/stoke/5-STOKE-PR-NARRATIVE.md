# Stoke: PR Launch Narrative

## Core Story

> Amateurs who train for big goals get two kinds of nutrition advice. Calorie apps count everything, which suits the job they were built for, weight loss. Sports nutrition apps hand over a plan that tells them exactly what to eat and when. Both ask people with day jobs to change everything. The science points somewhere smaller. Knowledge alone barely moves behavior: in a randomized trial, nutrition education raised young endurance athletes' knowledge but "was not enough to change dietary intake." What does move it is concrete: offering swaps improved food choices about three times as much as nutrition labels in one randomized trial, and helped beginners most. And the gaps that matter are few and specific: only 5.3% of marathoners in one 2025 study hit race carbohydrate targets, and amateurs who followed a science-based race strategy ran 4.7% faster. Stoke looks at what you already eat and offers one swap, backed by sports science, that does the most for your goal. It's not a meal plan. It's a swap.

### Why This Story Is Newsworthy

| Criteria | How Stoke Delivers |
|----------|--------------------|
| **The hook** | "You don't need a meal plan to eat like an athlete." It challenges both the calorie counter and the plan app, and it frees millions of amateurs who have been told that eating for a goal means living by numbers or a schedule. |
| **Candor** | Stoke concedes that calorie counting works for weight loss, that plans work for people who follow them, and that swap evidence comes from general-population trials with modest effects. It publishes its swap method and the limits of the evidence, so journalists can quote a startup that says what it cannot prove. |
| **Evidence-backed** | The ACSM/AND/DC joint position (2016), the ISSN protein stand (2017), the IOC consensus on REDs (2023), the knowledge-behavior trial (Heikkilä et al., 2019), randomized swap trials (Jansen et al., 2021; Schruff-Lim et al., 2024), habit formation (Lally et al., 2010) and amateur fueling studies (Hansen et al., 2014; Smyth, 2021; *Eur J Sport Sci*, 2025). Every claim stays inside them: Stoke says what each swap is built on, never that it makes anyone faster. |
| **Timely** | HYROX's growth and Infront's September 2026 sale of its majority stake to an L Catterton-led consortium, London's record ballot (May 2026), MyFitnessPal's purchase of Cal AI and its HYROX Tampa sponsorship (2026), Hexis's direct-to-consumer push (June 2026), GLP-1 drugs reshaping the diet industry, and Apple's iOS 27 food ratings (September 2026). |
| **Universal** | Every amateur has tried to "eat better" for a race, started a plan and dropped it when real life got in the way, been told a bagel is "bad," or hit the wall and wondered what went wrong. |

## The Narrative Arc (Three Acts)

**Act 1: The tools did their jobs (concede the status quo)**
"Calorie counting works for what it was built for. If you're trying to lose weight, logging what you eat helps, and the trials say so. Plans work too, for people who can live by them: pros with nutritionists, and athletes who want to be told exactly what to eat and when. Nobody needs to apologize for either."

**Act 2: Then the amateurs showed up (reveal the gap with evidence)**
"But look who's in the start pen now: a million and a half HYROX racers, 1.3 million people trying to get into London, a generation lifting weights. They have jobs, work lunches and kids' birthday parties. Most know little of the science: in one survey, fewer than 2 in 100 amateur endurance athletes knew how much carbohydrate refuels you fastest after a hard session. And knowing more isn't enough: in a trial, nutrition lessons raised athletes' knowledge but didn't change what they ate. So they under-fuel the sessions that matter: in one study, only about one marathoner in twenty hit their race carbohydrate target. What does change behavior is small and concrete. Offer people a specific swap and their choices improve, most of all for beginners."

**Act 3: Small swaps, big goals (deliver Stoke)**
"Stoke starts from the food you already eat. Snap your meal and it gives you one swap, backed by sports science, that does the most for your goal: protein at breakfast for the lifter, carbohydrate the night before the long run, both for the HYROX racer. No meal plan, no calorie budget, no scale. Meal plans tell you what to eat. Stoke makes what you already eat better for your goal, one swap at a time."

Same story at every scale: press pitch, podcast interview, 60-second social video.

## Shareability Check (STEPPS)

| STEPPS Lever | How Stoke's Story Delivers |
|--------------|----------------------------|
| **Social Currency** | Knowing the bagel-over-salad swap makes you the person in the run club who understands eating for a race. The playbook of principles you've earned, the "Stoked." card and beta access through a coach or club are insider badges that say "I prepare properly, without obsessing." |
| **Triggers** | Every meal photo; the night before a long run or heavy session, when the swap nudge arrives; the work lunch, the moment a meal plan breaks and a swap still works; race week; the gel in the pocket at km 25. |
| **Emotion** | High-arousal relief and indignation at the false choice ("I don't have time to live by a meal plan, and I'm not on a diet"), plus the surprise of swaps that add food. Never guilt, which is low-arousal and off-brand. |
| **Public** | The ember on shared weekly cards ("12 swaps this week") and race-week cards, "Post your swap" before race weekends, and swaps in Strava activity descriptions: visible, persistent residue after the launch. |
| **Practical Value** | One-pagers that travel without the app: "The Bagel Test" (one bagel, three goals, three swaps: honey for the long-run morning, eggs for the lifter's breakfast, jam instead of cream cheese two hours before HYROX intervals); "Race carbohydrate per hour" (30-60 g/h for 1-2.5 hours, up to 90 g/h beyond 2.5-3 hours); "Protein per meal" (about 0.25-0.4 g/kg). |
| **Stories** | The archetype: the amateur who downloaded a meal plan, dropped it in week two, then made one swap at the meals that mattered and finished strong. The swap is the plot; remove it and the story has no turning point. |

**Trojan horse test:** retold without naming Stoke, the story becomes "you don't need a meal plan; make one better swap to what you already eat." The claim survives, but the listener's next question is "which swap, for my goal and my week?", and answering it takes a goal, a training calendar and the guidelines: Stoke's idea. The Bagel Test cannot be told without that logic either: "the same bagel gets honey before a long run and eggs at a lifter's breakfast" is the One Swap in a sentence. Like Blendtec's "Will It Blend?", where the story cannot be told without the blender's power, the product's idea is the plot. **Pass.**

## Influencer Strategy

### Who to Target

| Target | Why | How |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **Sports dietitians (RD, CSSD) active on social** | Their audiences already accept practical, food-first advice, and their endorsement transfers clinical authority. Their review also protects the Energy Floor. | Advisory roles, swap-method review, co-authoring the evidence brief; paid for advisory work, never for posts. Their names on the method are the insider credential. |
| **HYROX affiliate-gym coaches and age-group racers** | They live the hybrid nutrition problem every week and talk to their members daily; HYROX counts about 15,000 affiliate gyms. | Early access for their members, a race-week swap clinic kit, first look at the HYROX data. |
| **Run-club leaders** | Clubs are where amateur marathoners learn; Strava counted 1 million clubs in 2025. | Club access codes, long-run swap nights, the Bagel Test one-pager. |
| **Evidence-minded amateur creators** (marathon, hybrid, lifting) | Credible because they are amateurs with day jobs, and their followers are the tribe. | Access plus the evidence one-pager, and the single request below; their swaps become public artifacts. |

### Who to Avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| **Meal-plan sellers and "what I eat in a day" plan creators** | The adjacent-posture purists: their product is the prescription Stoke positions against, and pairing with them blurs "one swap, not a plan." |
| **Weight-loss and "get lean" creators** | They tie Stoke to the diet paradigm, and their audiences track for the weight motive most linked to harm. |
| **Clean-eating and anti-ultra-processed purists** | They will attack context-based gel and bagel swaps; pairing with them invites a "Stoke endorses junk food" story. |
| **Low-carb, keto and carnivore advocates** | They reject carbohydrate for training outright, and the ISSN found ketogenic diets largely neutral or detrimental for performance (Leaf et al., 2024). |
| **Supplement hype accounts** | They undermine credibility, and Stoke sells no supplements; it must not look like a funnel for them. |
| **Paid ambassadors of MyFitnessPal's or Myprotein's HYROX programs** | Conflicted promoters; their posts would read as paid switching. |
| **Elite pros as the face** | It puts Stoke on Hexis's "used by the pros" ladder and away from the amateur tribe. |

### The Influencer Brief

- They receive three months of access, the evidence one-pager ("The Amateur Nutrition Gap") and the swap method.
- The narrative comes as context, not script: why Stoke exists (one swap to the food you already eat, not a plan) and what it will not claim.
- One request: "For the next two weeks, eat the way you normally do and snap it. When Stoke offers a swap you'd actually take, take it, and tell people how the next session went."
- No talking points. No hashtag requirements. The authentic content moment is the night before a hard session and the morning after it.

## What NOT to Do

| Mistake | Why It Fails |
|---------|-------------|
| **Drifting into a meal plan (the single most dangerous)** | Adding daily plans, meal schedules, portion prescriptions or "what to eat today" turns Stoke into a smaller, later Fuelin. It erases the only difference that holds when features converge, and it breaks the promise to people who won't live by a plan. In press, never name Fuelin: use the public form of the line, "Meal plans tell you what to eat. Stoke makes what you already eat better for your goal, one swap at a time." |
| **Leading with the score or with AI photo logging** | "The food score app" puts Stoke on the health-score ladder (Yuka, ZOE, Apple) and turns a learning device into a target; "snap to log" puts it on Cal AI's ladder, which MyFitnessPal owns. Lead with the swap. |
| **Advertising before the claim is established** | "You don't need a meal plan" is a contrarian claim that needs third parties (dietitians, journalists, coaches) to make it credible, and MyFitnessPal can outspend any startup. Ads without earned credibility read as a startup shouting. Red Bull and Starbucks were built by publicity first and advertising later. |
| **"Eat like a pro," "clean" or weight-loss framing** | "Pro" puts Stoke on Hexis's ladder and contradicts the amateur tribe; "clean" puts it on the health-score ladder and moralizes food; weight-loss language makes it a smaller MyFitnessPal and adopts the motive most associated with harm among app users. |
| **Praising low intake or gamifying restriction** | Under-fueling risk is common in recreational athletes, and athletes carry elevated eating-disorder rates. A game that rewarded eating less would turn Stoke's feedback into harm; the Energy Floor safeguards and the review of every game mechanic are launch requirements, not patches. |
| **The overclaim** | "Proven to make you faster," "swaps proven for athletes," "the most accurate," "the first" or invented HYROX science: one dietitian's quote ends the credibility position, as Cal AI's slide from "90% accurate" to "about 80%" shows. |
| **Ambushing HYROX or its sponsors** | Unofficial event marketing using HYROX marks invites legal and community backlash. Go through coaches, gyms and research instead. |
