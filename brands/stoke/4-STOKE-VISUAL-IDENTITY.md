# Stoke: Visual Identity

## Verbal Nail (Locked)

**"Fuel, not calories."** Food judged by the work ahead.

Every visual decision encodes this. Not "burn": the flame and the calories-burned counter belong to the budget Stoke rejects. Not "clean eating": leaves, salads and pastel greens belong to diet and health apps. Not "elite lab": graphs, navy data screens and pro athletes belong to the fueling plans. The feeling of being fired up and ready: the fuel is in, the work is next.

## Visual Hammer

### Primary Hammer: The Ember (a glowing coal that brightens when your plate fits the session ahead)

**Concept:** a single ember: a soft, rounded, faceted coal that glows from the inside. It is the logo mark, the app icon and the Fuel Score indicator in one. In the app, the ember glows hotter as a plate fits the session ahead and rests cool grey when it doesn't; it is never red and never an X. Its signature motion is "the stoke": when you take the One Swap, the ember draws a breath, brightens, and sends up three sparks. Like the Coca-Cola contour bottle or the Aflac duck, it is a concrete image rather than an abstract mark, so it carries meaning from the first day.

| Attribute | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **What it encodes** | "Fuel, not calories": energy stored and ready to burn, not energy already burned. A coal is fuel waiting for work, which is what a plate is the night before a long run. |
| **Emotional tone** | Warm, ready, fired up: the moment before a start. It must never produce guilt, alarm or the clinical chill of a lab result. |
| **Connection to the name** | Total. You stoke embers; the name is the action and the ember is its object. Name, symbol and position are one idea: feed the fire for the work ahead. |
| **Uniqueness in category** | Inventory: progress rings (Apple's Activity rings and the calorie-remaining rings common in tracker dashboards); traffic-light color codes (Hexis's green/amber/red Carb Coding, Fuelin's red/yellow/green carb guide, Nutri-Score's A to E); semicircular gauges (the visual language of wearable readiness and strain scores); chevrons (Strava); plain wordmarks (Cal AI). No food or fitness brand reviewed uses an ember. The space is empty. |
| **Swap test** | On MyFitnessPal, an ember contradicts the budget: it asks you to add fuel, not stay under a number. On Hexis or Fuelin it has no meaning inside a traffic-light system. On Strava it reads as a stray heat icon. It reads correctly only on Stoke. **Pass.** |
| **Reduction test** | At 32x32 px: a rounded crimson coal with a bright core on charcoal. In black and white: a faceted rounded silhouette with a lighter core, still distinct from a flame because it has no tongue or point. At 16 px the color carries it. |
| **Cultural precedent** | "Keep the embers alive" and banking a fire overnight are old, legible images of stored energy. Calcifer, the fire spirit in *Howl's Moving Castle* who must be fed to keep the castle moving, proves a living ember is lovable and instantly understood: borrowed familiarity from an unclaimed category. |
| **Animation/motion** | "The stoke": breath, brighten, three sparks on taking the One Swap. A slow ember "breathing" on loading screens. A bright flare with the word "Stoked." when a race-week plate hits. |

### The Symbol's Internal System

| Component | Maps To | Logic |
|-----------|---------|-------|
| **Cold** (charcoal, no glow) | The plate falls short of the session ahead (starting threshold: score under 50) | Shows the One Swap. Cool grey, never red: a cold coal is not a failure, just unfed |
| **Warm** (crimson glow) | The plate fits the session (50-79) | Fuel on track |
| **Hot** (crimson edge, bright core) | The plate is ideal for the session (80+) | Earns the "Stoked." flare |
| **Banked** (low, steady glow) | A rest day | Recovery is part of training; a banked fire is a healthy fire |
| **Fading** (glow dimming across days) | The Fuel Floor: intake running low against training load | A warm, specific prompt to add fuel; never praise for eating less |

Score thresholds are starting points for design and must be validated with users and the advisory board.

### Candidates Evaluated and Rejected

**The flame.** Tempting because it is the obvious picture of "stoke." It fails because a flame commonly stands for "calories burned" in fitness apps, so it would encode the exact budget Stoke rejects, and it would pass the swap test onto any calorie app. **Discarded.**

**The fuel gauge needle.** Tempting because "E to F" literally says fuel. It fails because semicircular gauges already read as readiness and strain on wearables, so it collapses into that category's visual language, and it carries petrol associations. **Discarded.**

**A traffic-light plate.** Tempting because color-coding a plate is instantly legible. It fails because it is the category cliché (Hexis, Fuelin, Nutri-Score), it moralizes food as good or bad, and red would mark the very sports foods Stoke scores correctly in context. **Discarded.**

**The race bib.** Tempting because the pinned number is the badge of the ambitious amateur. It fails because it excludes lifters, encodes "race" rather than "fuel," and belongs to every race organizer. **Discarded.**

**The lightning bolt.** Tempting because it reads as energy. It fails because it is Gatorade's hammer in sports fuel. **Discarded.**

**The four-point AI sparkle.** Tempting as shorthand for "smart." It fails because it is the generic AI icon and puts the mechanism, which competitors share, ahead of the position. **Discarded.**

## Color

### Primary Brand Color: Stoke Crimson

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Tone alignment** | The color of a coal when you blow on it: heat, appetite and readiness. It says energy is coming, which is what "fuel" means. |
| **Category contrast** | Strava = orange (#FC5200). Cronometer = orange (#FF6734). MyFitnessPal = blue (#0066EE). Hexis = blue (#359CEF) with navy. HYROX = yellow (#FFED00) on black. Fuelin = yellow (#FFCC00). Yazio = mint (#09DAA9). Runna = near-black (#161616). Cal AI = black and white. ZOE = muted beige and ochre. Crimson is unoccupied. |
| **Functional role** | Reserved for fueled states: the ember's glow, a plate that fits, the share card, primary actions. It is the color of "you're ready," never of a warning. |
| **Emotional signal** | "Hot, ready, alive," not "danger," "stop" or "diet." |

Competitor colors above were read from each brand's own website code on 5 October 2026 (the dominant brand color or declared theme color).

**Specific direction:** a pinkish crimson, starting around #E0245E, the color of the brightest part of a coal bed rather than of a flame. Avoid fire-engine red (#FF0000 reads as error and stop), avoid any drift toward orange (Strava's territory), and avoid magenta-purple (reads beauty and wellness). Test the shade on charcoal and on warm off-white; it must glow on dark and stay rich on light. Then never change it: Tiffany's blue and UPS's brown became hammers through decades of identical use.

### Color Usage Rule: The Heat Gradient Means Fuel

The crimson-to-core heat gradient appears only when food fits the session ahead, and on brand marks. Low scores, errors and warnings never use crimson; they use cool grey and plain language. If crimson ever signals a problem, the hammer stops meaning "fueled," and every competitor's red-for-bad convention swallows it.

### Colors Rejected

- **Orange:** Strava's and Cronometer's territory; the athlete's mind already files orange under Strava.
- **Yellow or amber as a primary:** HYROX (yellow on black) and Fuelin; amber is also Hexis's "moderate carb" signal.
- **Blue:** MyFitnessPal and Hexis; reads as the calorie counter or the lab.
- **Green and mint:** Yazio's mint, and "good" in Nutri-Score's grades; green would moralize food.
- **Black-only premium:** Cal AI, Runna and HYROX already use black as their premium signal; it is a commodity in this category.
- **Pure red:** reads as error and stop, and it is the low-carbohydrate color in the traffic-light systems.

### Supporting Palette

- Stoke Crimson, starting at #E0245E (primary: fueled states, marks, primary actions)
- Charcoal, around #16161A (backgrounds: the coal the glow comes out of)
- Ash, around #EDEAE6 (light-mode surfaces)
- Core Glow, around #FFC98A (accent, reserved: only inside the ember's heat gradient, never a standalone color, so it never drifts toward HYROX or Fuelin yellow)
- Cool Grey, around #8B8B94 (cold and neutral states: plates that need a swap, inactive elements)
- Bone, around #F4F1EC, on dark; Coal Text, around #1C1C21, on light (text)

## Typography

| Element | Direction |
|---------|-----------|
| **Wordmark style** | A heavy, slightly rounded grotesk with tight, confident spacing: athletic but warm, an amateur's brand rather than a lab's. |
| **Case** | Lowercase: "stoke" |
| **Reference feel** | Nike's athletic confidence meets Monzo's plain-spoken warmth. Scores set in tabular, condensed numerals that read like a race clock. |
| **Avoid** | Thin geometric sans and pastels (diet and wellness apps: reads "calorie counting"); monospace or sci-fi tech faces (the elite-lab look of the fueling plans); stencil or aggressive slab faces (gym-bro, supplement shelves); scripts (lifestyle, not training). |

## App Icon / Primary Mark

**A single ember glowing from a crimson edge to a warm core, centered on a charcoal tile, with no text.**

### Reduction Test

| Size | What Should Be Visible |
|------|----------------------|
| App icon (60x60) | The ember's rounded, faceted silhouette and its crimson-to-core glow |
| Favicon (32x32) | A rounded crimson ember with a bright core on charcoal |
| Social avatar (16x16) | A hot crimson point on charcoal: color and contrast carry recognition |

## Design Language (In-App & Marketing)

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Coal and glow** | Dark charcoal surfaces; warmth appears only when it is earned. Avoids the diet-app cliché of white screens, pastel greens and salad photography. |
| **The ember is everywhere** | App icon, Fuel Score indicator, loading state (a breathing ember), the night-before nudge, the race-week card and the share card. One symbol, repeated for years. |
| **Session first** | The next session leads every screen ("Tomorrow: 30 km long run"), then the Fuel Score, then the One Swap. No calorie number on the home screen; macros sit one tap deeper. The differentiator leads; the mechanism hides. |
| **Celebrate fuel, never punish** | A plate that fits makes the ember flare and says "Stoked." A plate that falls short turns the ember cool grey and offers one swap. Never red, never an X, never "over budget." |
| **Inform, don't alarm** | Fuel Floor messages are warm and specific ("Your last three hard days ran light on fuel. Add a snack before tomorrow's intervals.") with a quiet link to professional help. No shame, no countdowns, no numbers framed as limits. |
| **Real amateurs, real food** | Photography of people at 6 am before work, sweat and breath in cold air, bagels, rice bowls, leftovers, a gel in a pocket. Never shredded models, before-and-after bodies, scales, measuring tapes or salad flat-lays. |

                 ---------------------------------------
