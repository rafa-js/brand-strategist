# Stoke: Visual Identity

## Verbal Nail (Locked)

**"Small swaps. Big goals."** Small changes, made consistently, reach big goals.

Every visual decision encodes this. Not "the plan": calendars, meal grids, schedules and checklists belong to the daily plans of Fuelin and Hexis. Not "burn" or "budget": the flame and the calories-remaining ring belong to the calorie trackers. Not "clean eating": leaves, salads and pastel greens belong to diet and health apps. Not "the score" as a prize: Stoke's score is feedback, so no trophies, grades or leaderboards. The feeling of momentum: each small swap makes the fire a little hotter and the goal a little closer.

## Visual Hammer

### Primary Hammer: The Ember (a glowing coal that flares each time you take a swap)

**Concept:** a single ember: a soft, rounded, faceted coal that glows from the inside. It is the logo mark, the app icon and the in-app feedback in one. Every swap you take stokes it: the ember draws a breath, brightens and sends up three sparks. Across the week, its glow shows how well your meals have served your goal; when a meal could do more, it rests cool grey beside the One Swap, never red and never an X. Like the Coca-Cola contour bottle or the Aflac duck, it is a concrete image rather than an abstract mark, so it carries meaning from the first day.

| Attribute | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **What it encodes** | "Small swaps. Big goals.": nobody keeps a fire going with one giant log; it stays alive through small, regular stokes. Each swap is a stoke, and the fire is the goal you are keeping alive. |
| **Emotional tone** | Warm, encouraging, building: the feeling of momentum. It must never produce guilt, alarm, the chill of a lab result or the judgment of a grade. |
| **Connection to the name** | Total. You stoke embers: the name is the action (taking the swap) and the ember is its object (your fire). Name, symbol and position are one idea: small stokes keep a big fire burning. |
| **Uniqueness in category** | Inventory: progress rings (Apple's Activity rings and the calorie-remaining rings common in tracker dashboards); traffic-light color codes (Hexis's green/amber/red Carb Coding, Fuelin's red/yellow/green carb guide, Nutri-Score's A to E); semicircular gauges (the visual language of wearable readiness and strain scores); chevrons (Strava); plain wordmarks (Cal AI); and the generic two-arrow exchange icon of the healthy-swap scanners. No food or fitness brand reviewed uses an ember. The space is empty. |
| **Swap test** | On Fuelin or Hexis, an ember has no meaning inside a plan and a traffic-light system. On MyFitnessPal it contradicts the budget: it asks you to stoke a fire, not to stay under a number. On Strava it reads as a stray heat icon. It reads correctly only on Stoke. **Pass.** |
| **Reduction test** | At 32x32 px: a rounded crimson coal with a bright core on charcoal. In black and white: a faceted rounded silhouette with a lighter core, still distinct from a flame because it has no tongue or point. At 16 px the color carries it. |
| **Cultural precedent** | "Keep the embers alive" and banking a fire overnight are old, legible images of small, steady care producing lasting heat. Calcifer, the fire spirit in *Howl's Moving Castle* who must be fed to keep the castle moving, proves a living ember is lovable and instantly understood: borrowed familiarity from an unclaimed category. |
| **Animation/motion** | "The stoke": breath, brighten, three sparks each time a swap is taken. A slow ember "breathing" on loading screens. A bright flare with the word "Stoked." when the week's glow reaches its peak or race-week swaps are complete. |

### The Symbol's Internal System

| Component | Maps To | Logic |
|-----------|---------|-------|
| **Cold** (charcoal, no glow) | The meal could do more for the goal (starting threshold: meal score under 50) | Shows the One Swap. Cool grey, never red: a cold coal is not a failure, just waiting for a stoke |
| **Warm** (crimson glow) | The meal serves the goal (50-79) | On track |
| **Hot** (crimson edge, bright core) | The meal is ideal for the goal, or the swap was taken (80+) | Earns the stoke animation |
| **Banked** (low, steady glow) | A rest day | Recovery is part of training; a banked fire is a healthy fire, and rest never breaks a streak |
| **Fading** (glow dimming across days) | The Energy Floor: intake running low against training load | A warm, specific prompt to add food; never praise for eating less |

Score thresholds are starting points for design and must be validated with users and the advisory board. The score is shown as the ember's heat first and as a small number second, never as a hero figure.

### Candidates Evaluated and Rejected

**The flame.** Tempting because it is the obvious picture of "stoke." It fails because a flame commonly stands for "calories burned" in fitness apps, so it would encode the budget Stoke rejects, and it would pass the swap test onto any calorie app. **Discarded.**

**The swap arrows.** Tempting because two curved arrows literally say "swap." It fails because it is the generic exchange and refresh icon, already used by the healthy-swap scanners, and it pictures the mechanism instead of the feeling (momentum toward a goal). **Discarded.**

**A meal calendar or checklist.** Tempting because it signals structure and progress. It fails because it is the visual language of the daily plan, the exact opposite Stoke stands against, and it would make Stoke look like a smaller Fuelin. **Discarded.**

**The fuel gauge needle.** Tempting because "E to F" reads as energy. It fails because semicircular gauges already read as readiness and strain on wearables, and "fuel" is the root of Fuelin's name. **Discarded.**

**A traffic-light plate.** Tempting because color-coding a plate is instantly legible. It fails because it is the category cliché (Hexis, Fuelin, Nutri-Score), it moralizes food as good or bad, and red would mark the very sports foods Stoke recommends in context. **Discarded.**

**The race bib.** Tempting because the pinned number is the badge of the ambitious amateur. It fails because it excludes lifters, encodes "race" rather than "small swaps," and belongs to every race organizer. **Discarded.**

**The lightning bolt.** Tempting because it reads as energy. It fails because it is Gatorade's hammer in sports nutrition. **Discarded.**

**The four-point AI sparkle.** Tempting as shorthand for "smart." It fails because it is the generic AI icon and puts the mechanism, which competitors share, ahead of the position. **Discarded.**

## Color

### Primary Brand Color: Stoke Crimson

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Tone alignment** | The color of a coal when you blow on it: warmth, appetite and momentum. It says the fire is building, which is what a run of small swaps feels like. |
| **Category contrast** | Strava = orange (#FC5200). Cronometer = orange (#FF6734). MyFitnessPal = blue (#0066EE). Hexis = blue (#359CEF) with navy. HYROX = yellow (#FFED00) on black. Fuelin = yellow (#FFCC00). Yazio = mint (#09DAA9). Runna = near-black (#161616). Cal AI = black and white. ZOE = muted beige and ochre. Crimson is unoccupied. |
| **Functional role** | Reserved for progress: the ember's glow, a swap taken, the share card, primary actions. It is the color of "that's working," never of a warning. |
| **Emotional signal** | "Warm, alive, building," not "danger," "stop" or "diet." |

Competitor colors above were read from each brand's own website code on 5 October 2026 (the dominant brand color or declared theme color).

**Specific direction:** a pinkish crimson, starting around #E0245E, the color of the brightest part of a coal bed rather than of a flame. Avoid fire-engine red (#FF0000 reads as error and stop), avoid any drift toward orange (Strava's territory) or yellow (Fuelin's), and avoid magenta-purple (reads beauty and wellness). Test the shade on charcoal and on warm off-white; it must glow on dark and stay rich on light. Then never change it: Tiffany's blue and UPS's brown became hammers through decades of identical use.

### Color Usage Rule: The Heat Gradient Means Progress

The crimson-to-core heat gradient appears only when a meal serves the goal or a swap is taken, and on brand marks. Meals that could do more, errors and warnings never use crimson; they use cool grey and plain language. If crimson ever signals a problem, the hammer stops meaning "that's working," and every competitor's red-for-bad convention swallows it.

### Colors Rejected

- **Orange:** Strava's and Cronometer's territory; the athlete's mind already files orange under Strava.
- **Yellow or amber as a primary:** Fuelin (#FFCC00) and HYROX (yellow on black); amber is also Hexis's "moderate carb" signal. Any yellow would put Stoke beside its closest competitor.
- **Blue:** MyFitnessPal and Hexis; reads as the calorie counter or the lab.
- **Green and mint:** Yazio's mint, and "good" in Nutri-Score's grades; green would moralize food.
- **Black-only premium:** Cal AI, Runna and HYROX already use black as their premium signal; it is a commodity in this category.
- **Pure red:** reads as error and stop, and it is the low-carbohydrate color in the traffic-light systems.

### Supporting Palette

- Stoke Crimson, starting at #E0245E (primary: progress states, marks, primary actions)
- Charcoal, around #16161A (backgrounds: the coal the glow comes out of)
- Ash, around #EDEAE6 (light-mode surfaces)
- Core Glow, around #FFC98A (accent, reserved: only inside the ember's heat gradient, never a standalone color, so it never drifts toward Fuelin or HYROX yellow)
- Cool Grey, around #8B8B94 (cold and neutral states: meals waiting for a swap, inactive elements)
- Bone, around #F4F1EC, on dark; Coal Text, around #1C1C21, on light (text)

## Typography

| Element | Direction |
|---------|-----------|
| **Wordmark style** | A heavy, slightly rounded grotesk with tight, confident spacing: athletic but warm, an amateur's brand rather than a lab's. |
| **Case** | Lowercase: "stoke" |
| **Reference feel** | Nike's athletic confidence meets Monzo's plain-spoken warmth. The swap is set large in plain words; scores are set small, in tabular condensed numerals that read like a race clock. |
| **Avoid** | Thin geometric sans and pastels (diet and wellness apps: reads "calorie counting"); monospace or sci-fi tech faces (the elite-lab look of the plan apps); stencil or aggressive slab faces (gym-bro, supplement shelves); scripts (lifestyle, not training). |

## App Icon / Primary Mark

**A single ember glowing from a crimson edge to a warm core, centered on a charcoal tile, with no text.**

### Reduction Test

| Size | What Should Be Visible |
|------|----------------------|
| App icon (60x60) | The ember's rounded, faceted silhouette and its crimson-to-core glow |
| Favicon (32x32) | A rounded crimson ember with a bright core on charcoal |
| Social avatar (16x16) | A hot crimson point on charcoal: color and contrast carry recognition |

## Design Language (In-App & Marketing)

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Coal and glow** | Dark charcoal surfaces; warmth appears only when it is earned. Avoids the diet-app cliché of white screens, pastel greens and salad photography, and the plan apps' grids of meals and macros. |
| **The ember is everywhere** | App icon, the stoke on every swap, the week's glow, the loading state (a breathing ember), the night-before nudge, the race-week card and the share card. One symbol, repeated for years. |
| **Swap first, score second** | Every meal screen leads with the One Swap and its one line of why ("Tomorrow: 30 km. Swap the salad for a rice bowl."). The ember's glow shows the meal score; the number sits small beside it; macros sit one tap deeper. No daily targets, no meal calendar, no plan view. The differentiator leads; the feedback supports; the mechanism hides. |
| **Celebrate swaps, never punish** | Taking a swap stokes the ember and, at the week's peak, earns "Stoked." A meal that could do more turns the ember cool grey and offers one swap. Never red, never an X, never "over budget," and rest days bank the fire instead of breaking a streak. |
| **Inform, don't alarm** | Energy Floor messages are warm and specific ("Your last three hard days ran light. Add a snack before tomorrow's intervals.") with a quiet link to professional help. No shame, no countdowns, no numbers framed as limits. |
| **Real amateurs, real food** | Photography of people at 6 am before work, sweat and breath in cold air, work lunches, family dinners, bagels, rice bowls, leftovers, a gel in a pocket: the meals no plan controls. Never shredded models, before-and-after bodies, scales, measuring tapes, meal-prep grids or salad flat-lays. |

## The Integrated System

```
Verbal Nail:    "Small swaps. Big goals."
Visual Hammer:   The Ember (a glowing coal that flares each time you take a swap)
Color Hammer:    Stoke Crimson (unoccupied: orange, blue, yellow, mint and black are taken)
Typography:      Heavy rounded grotesk, lowercase "stoke"
                 ---------------------------------------
                 = A brand that feels like the training
                   partner who never hands you a meal
                   plan: they look at what's on your
                   plate, suggest one better swap for
                   your goal, explain why in a sentence,
                   and get stoked with you as it adds up.
```
