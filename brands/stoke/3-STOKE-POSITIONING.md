# Stoke: Positioning

## The Word to Own

**Fuel.**

Not "calories": that word belongs to MyFitnessPal, whose App Store name is "Calorie Counter," and to Cal AI, which carries it in its name. Not "macros": MacroFactor owns the smart-macro position among lifters. Not "health": Yuka, ZOE, Nutri-Score and now Apple's iOS 27 grade food for everyone's long-term health. Not "performance": wearables already sell it as recovery and readiness scores. **Fuel.** The question Stoke answers is not "how many calories did I eat?" or "is this food healthy?": it is **"will what I'm eating fuel what I'm training for next?"** The niche fueling apps use the word too, but as a plan they hand down (Hexis's carb codes, Fuelin's targets), and none has put it in the amateur's mind: the biggest of them has about 1,300 US App Store ratings. The calorie leaders cannot take the word at all without contradicting their own names, because fuel is the one thing a calorie budget punishes: eating more before the work that needs it. Stoke will own it the way Volvo owns safety: by saying one thing longer and more consistently than anyone else.

## Positioning Architecture

### Onliness Statement (the differentiation test)

> **Stoke is the only food tracker that scores the food you actually eat against your goal and your next session, and gives you one swap instead of a meal plan.**

- **WHAT** (category): the fuel tracker, a new branch of food tracking that judges food by the training ahead
- **HOW** (the only): a food-level score that changes with the goal and the next session, plus one swap of the food you chose. Hexis and Fuelin code meals by carbohydrate amount and plan ahead; health scores ignore training; calorie trackers count against a budget
- **WHO** (audience): ambitious amateurs who train for a result (a race time, a heavier lift, a HYROX finish) around a day job: not dieters, not pros
- **WHERE** (market): English-language app stores, US and UK first, reached through HYROX affiliate gyms, run clubs and strength gyms
- **WHY** (need state): they train like athletes but have been handed dieting tools or pro meal plans; most under-fuel the sessions that matter (only 5.3% of marathoners in one 2025 study hit race carbohydrate targets) and don't know their protein needs (53% of Americans don't; IFIC, 2025)
- **WHEN** (trend): the mass-participation wave in goal-driven amateur sport (HYROX reports 1.4-1.5 million participants in 2025/26; London drew 1.34 million ballot applications for 2027; Gen Z leads gym growth), while the calorie category re-tools around GLP-1 weight loss

### Single-Sentence Position (External)

> **Stoke is the fuel tracker that scores food for the training ahead, for amateurs who train for a result, not a weight.**

- Identifies the prospect by attitude: "amateurs who train for a result, not a weight" captures the HYROX racer, the marathoner chasing a time and the lifter chasing a number, and excludes the dieter. The group is large and reachable: HYROX counts about 15,000 affiliate gyms (SportsPro, 2026), Strava 1 million clubs (Strava, 2025), and 81 million Americans belong to a gym (HFA, 2026).
- Repositions the competition: calorie apps become tools for a weight, health scores tools for general health, fueling plans tools for pros.
- States what Stoke IS (a fuel tracker), not what it does.
- Passes the opposite test: "the calorie counter for people trying to lose weight" is a real, viable position, held by the top-grossing app in the category, MyFitnessPal: Calorie Counter.

### Tagline

**Fuel, not calories.**

- The move: a contrarian substitution in three words. 7-Up became "the Uncola" by defining itself against the category leader; Stoke defines itself against the calorie, naming the enemy and the alternative at once.
- The law: the Law of the Opposite, applied through the Law of Attributes. The leader owns "calories," so the challenger owns the opposite attribute instead of imitating it.
- Hostile-scrutiny check: a sports dietitian will object that energy availability, the sport's own under-fueling measure, is counted in calories. Correct, and Stoke agrees: it watches energy as a floor (the Fuel Floor) and rejects only the calorie budget as the scoreboard. The tagline is a stance on the unit of judgment, not a claim that energy doesn't matter, and the language discipline below keeps it that way.

### Expanded Position (Internal: for teams, briefs, decks)

> Stoke is the fuel tracker: the food tracker for people who train for something. Snap what you eat and Stoke scores it, 0 to 100, against your goal and the session you have next, using the ACSM, ISSN and IOC guidance: protein for the lifter, carbohydrate for the long run, both for the hybrid racer, all timed to the work. When a plate falls short, Stoke gives you one swap, never a meal plan. Over the weeks it learns your training rhythm and looks further ahead, to race week. Energy is watched as a floor that protects training, never budgeted as a ceiling. We believe food is judged by the work it does.

### Elevator Pitch

> "If you train for something, a marathon, a HYROX, a bigger squat, you've probably tried a calorie app. They're mostly built to help you lose weight. But the bagel a health app grades 'average' is exactly right the morning of a long run, and the sports-nutrition guidelines say so. Stoke scores what you actually eat against the session you've got next, and gives you one swap, not a meal plan. Snap your plate. Get fueled. Fuel, not calories."

## The Core Argument

**Calorie trackers are not wrong; they were built for a different job, and for people who train they hide the decisions that matter most.**

Calorie counting works for what it was built for: digital self-monitoring was linked to weight loss in 74% of reported occurrences across 39 trials (Patel et al., 2021), and careful logging gets daily energy within a few percent (Diktas et al., 2025). But the athlete's guidelines ask a different question. The ACSM/AND/DC joint position says "nutrition goals and requirements are not static," and that nutrition should follow "the needs of daily training sessions," timed "in relation to sport rather than general daily targets" (Thomas, Erdman and Burke, 2016). A daily budget cannot see that tomorrow is a 30 km run. A per-100 g health score cannot see it either, and Nutri-Score's own owner says its grade was built for "the needs of the general population." So amateurs get graded on the wrong test: the plain bagel that suits a long-run morning is "average," the gel that keeps them off the wall is "ultra-processed," and nothing in the app notices that 43-47% of recreational endurance athletes screen at risk of under-fueling (Henninger et al., 2024; Lane et al., 2019). Stoke doesn't count better. It asks the athlete's question.

**This is not a better calorie counter. This is a fuel tracker.**

## Language Discipline

### Always Say

| Phrase | Why |
|--------|-----|
| "fuel tracker" | Category ownership |
| "fuel," "fueled," "fuel for the work ahead" | Core concept |
| "Fuel Score" | Branded asset |
| "Food is judged by the work it does." | The fundamental belief |
| "the One Swap" | Signature language |
| "the session ahead," "your next session" | The context that makes Stoke the only |
| "a fuel floor, not a calorie ceiling" | The honest stance on energy |

### Never Say

| Phrase | Why |
|--------|-----|
| "calorie counter," "calorie tracker," "diet app" (as self-description) | Puts Stoke on MyFitnessPal's ladder |
| "lose weight," "cut," "lean out," "shred," "burn" | Drags Stoke into the weight paradigm and the motive most linked to disordered behavior |
| "the most accurate," "X% accurate," "proven to make you faster," "the first" | False or fragile; a single expert quote ends the credibility position |
| "clean," "junk," "good food," "bad food," "cheat meal" | Moralizes food, and makes context-scored gels look like endorsements of junk |
| "AI-powered calorie tracking," "snap to count" (as the lead) | The mechanism is not the position; it is Cal AI's ladder |
| "eat like a pro," "used by the pros" | Hexis's ladder; contradicts the amateur tribe |
| "calories don't matter" | False: energy matters as a floor, and saying otherwise fails the honesty test |
| "HYROX science," "the HYROX diet" | No published HYROX fueling research exists, and it implies an affiliation with the HYROX brand |
